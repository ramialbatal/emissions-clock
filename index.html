How this is calculated

Official inventories report emissions per year, not per second. When this page opens it downloads the latest annual figures for (currently up to ), projects them to using each country's latest year-on-year change (→), and spreads the result evenly over the year:

growth   = E[latest] / E[latest − 1] − 1
annual   = E[latest] × (1 + growth)^(this year − latest)
per sec  = annual ÷ seconds in this year
so far   = annual × fraction of the year elapsed (UTC)

Real emissions vary by season, weekday and time of day, so the live counters are a model, not a measurement. Germany is shown separately but is also part of the EU.

What the CO₂ figures cover: territorial carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels and making cement. Land-use change (such as deforestation) is excluded, and international aviation and shipping only appear in the world total.

What the methane figures cover: methane (CH₄) from all human sources — fossil fuel production and leaks, livestock, rice farming, landfills, and land use and fires. Tonnes of methane are converted to CO₂-equivalents (CO₂e): the amount of CO₂ that would cause the same warming over 100 years (one tonne of methane ≈ 27–30 t CO₂e). Methane only lasts about 12 years in the air but traps far more heat while it does, so cutting it is the fastest way to slow warming in the near term. Official methane data is published with a longer delay than CO₂, so its projection reaches further ahead.

The small line chart on each card shows that country's official annual emissions from to , with the dot marking the latest year and the percentage giving the change over the whole period. Each chart is scaled to its own minimum and maximum (it does not start at zero), so compare the shape of the trend rather than the heights between countries.
